Section 1

H–D–R – First Differentiation of Form

This HTML module presents the first visual representation of the relational sequence introduced in S1. META.

It is the earliest stage at which the original spherical potential becomes visibly differentiated through the first application of the H–D–R operator.

At this stage no structural system has yet been established.

No coordinate system exists.

No Vesica Piscis has yet been formed.

No extractable geometry is available.

The rendering therefore illustrates the transition from undifferentiated potential to the first observable relational form.

Rather than introducing a new object, the first H–D–R operation establishes the earliest self-relation within the original spherical potential.

This produces a relational triad consisting of:

  • the original spherical potential,
  • two equivalent spherical forms,
  • and the relational field established between them.

The two generated spheres remain in a halving–halving relation to one another while simultaneously remaining in the same relational proportion to the original spherical potential.

Accordingly, the rendering visualizes the first relational differentiation rather than the first structure.

Within the RP9 methodology, this distinction is fundamental.

Form precedes structure.

The first differentiation establishes a new relational form, but the positional references required for explicit structure have not yet emerged.

Only after this differentiated form undergoes further relational development does the framework proceed toward the first extractable structural configuration.

For this reason, the module should be understood as the visual beginning of the entire S1 sequence.

It illustrates the first observable consequence of the H–D–R operator before geometry, measurement, mathematical notation, or structural extraction become possible.

Section 1

The four rendering steps

STEP 0 — CIRCLES

Potential symmetry

The rendering starts with two identical circles in a relational state. Here, only the original potential is shown before any explicit structure has been extracted.

What is observed is not lines, coordinates or geometry, but a symmetrical relationship where both shapes exist simultaneously. This corresponds to the first operator exposure of the potential sphere through HDR.

No structure has been isolated yet.

Only the potential relationship is visible.

STEP 1 — INTERSECTION POINTS

The relationship becomes observable

When the circles are allowed to interact, their common intersection points arise.

This is the first explicit relation.

The rendering does not show a newly created structure, but the points where the previously latent relation becomes observable.

Here the first differentiation between potential and observable structure takes place.

The relation now has defined reference points.

STEP 2 — GROUPED LINES

The latent structure is exposed

When the intersections are connected, the first structural relations emerge.

What previously existed only as potential relations now become organized line systems.

The lines do not introduce any new information.

They only visualize the order that was already implicit in the original potential.

It is only here that direction, orientation and geometric organization can be observed.

STEP 3 — GROUPED DIAGONALS

The complete structural projection

In the final step, the line system is supplemented with diagonal relations.

Now the coherent structure that has been latent since the beginning emerges.

The diagonals act as the relational links that tie the entire system together into a unified structure.

This is not a new construction.

It is the most explicit projection of the same underlying relational potential that was already shown in STEP 0.

Here the complete structural organization becomes observable.

Relation to the HDR operator

This rendering illustrates how the potential structure is successively exposed through repeated applications of the HDR operator.

HDR does not act as a generator of structure.

HDR acts as the operation that makes the latent structure explicit through successive relational transformations. This is consistent with the operator formulation where HDR is described as a projection from latent to explicit structure, rather than a creative process.

Five successive HDR interactions

The rendering can therefore be read as five successive operations on the original potential sphere:

  1. HDR 1 exposes the first relational symmetry and the two overlapping circles.
  2. HDR 2 makes the intersections observable as the first explicit relation.
  3. HDR 3 organizes these relations into a first line system.
  4. HDR 4 connects the lines into a larger structural organization.
  5. HDR 5 completes the diagonal relations of the structure so that the entire latent geometry becomes explicit.

Each step builds on the previous step without introducing new information. The only thing that changes is the degree of explicit structure. The underlying relation remains the same throughout the sequence, while the observable organization gradually develops. This is in line with RP9's description of the HDR sequence as an iterative exposure of an invariant structure.

Section 2

Core4 – Scale Independent System

Two scales – one structure

This rendering shows the same final Core4 structure at two different scales simultaneously.

One represents a microscopic manifestation and the other a macroscopic manifestation, but both are based on exactly the same relational organization.

Therefore, it is not two different structures that are shown.

It is the same structure observed at two different scale levels.

HDR is scale independent

The HDR operator does not depend on any absolute size.

The operator acts on relationships between forms and not on their physical dimensions.

When the same operator is applied to a smaller or larger manifestation, the same underlying structure is therefore exposed.

What changes is only the scale of the representation.

The relational organization remains unchanged.

The structure is reproduced without change

The rendering shows that the structure established in the previous visualization can reappear at a new scale without the need to create any new structure.

The Micro and Macro systems therefore function as two simultaneous manifestations of the same relational principle.

This means that HDR does not produce a new structure for each scale.

The operator reproduces the same relational organization regardless of the level of observation.

This follows the methodological sequence where the relationship is established first and can then be projected recursively into successive representations without changing the origin of the structure.

Common relational continuity

Although the two systems have different sizes, they share the same:

  • center,

  • symmetries,

  • relational directions,

  • diagonal organization,

  • and structural function.

The difference between them is therefore not structural but only representational.

The rendering illustrates how the same relational organization can exist simultaneously at multiple levels of observation without losing its identity.

Scale-independent manifestation

The purpose of this visualization is not to compare two different geometric objects.

The purpose is to show that the underlying structure exposed by HDR is scale-independent.

Once the relationship has been established, the same organization can be manifested in both smaller and larger representations, while each relationship, symmetry, and orientation remains compatible with the original structure.

The rendering thus illustrates that HDR acts on the relational organization and not on the absolute size of the form being observed. This is consistent with RP9's methodological description of recursive propagation, where established structure is projected into successive representations without the need to create a new structural foundation.

Section 3

3. B5/B6 – Invariant Structure

The final rendering visualizes the relational state reached after the complete structural filtering process described throughout the previous sequences.

Unlike the earlier renderings, which demonstrate how the relational structure is progressively exposed, this visualization focuses on the unique observation state that remains after all structural requirements have been applied.

The rendering displays B5 and B6 as two opposing manifestations of the same invariant structure.

Although the form changes between the two states, the underlying relational organization remains unchanged.

The center remains fixed.

The intersection positions remain fixed.

The relational structure remains fixed.

Only the observable form changes.

This illustrates one of the central principles of RP9:

Form may vary while structure remains invariant.

During the animation the structure can move continuously between B5 and B6, demonstrating that structural variation does not necessarily imply structural change.

The invariant reference points remain constant throughout the transformation.

An equally important observation is that every invariant intersection is constrained to one of three orthogonal relational axes: X, Y and Z.

Throughout the entire selection process, all candidate configurations that failed to preserve these invariant conditions were eliminated.

Only B5 and B6 simultaneously satisfy every structural requirement:

  • invariant center
  • invariant intersections
  • three-line relational organization (X, Y, Z)
  • balanced differentiation
  • structural invariance during transformation

For this reason, B5 and B6 are not presented as arbitrary geometric positions, but as the only surviving candidates after the complete relational filtering process.

This rendering therefore serves as a visual verification of the invariant structure described throughout D. Logic 12, particularly the sections 12.B – Why B5 and B6 Survive and 12.C – Z = 0 and Balanced Asymmetry, where the complete relational selection process is explained in greater detail.

Section 4

4. B5/B6 – Interactive Image Comparator

The final HTML module extends the previous B5/B6 visualization by providing an interactive comparison environment.

Unlike the previous rendering, which demonstrates the invariant relationship between B5 and B6 through animation, this module allows the observer to examine the two states directly using a variety of image comparison techniques.

The purpose is not to introduce any new structure.

The purpose is to allow the already established structure to be examined from multiple visual perspectives.

Several comparison modes are available, including:

  • Fade
  • Difference
  • Highlight
  • Swipe
  • Checkerboard
  • Blink

Together with adjustable controls for mixing, contrast, threshold, gamma and zoom, these tools allow individual structural details to be isolated and compared interactively.

The central question is therefore not whether the two images appear identical.

The central question is whether the underlying relational structure remains invariant while the observable form changes.

Throughout every comparison mode, the observer can investigate whether the following remain unchanged:

  • the structural center
  • the invariant intersections
  • the three relational axes (X, Y and Z)
  • the overall relational organization

The module is intended as a visual laboratory rather than a presentation.

It allows the observer to independently inspect the relationship between B5 and B6 and verify that the invariant structure is preserved regardless of how the two representations are compared.

This directly reflects one of the central principles established throughout Logic 12:

Structure is invariant. Representation is not.

Accordingly, the comparison tools do not attempt to demonstrate that B5 and B6 are visually identical.

Instead, they provide multiple methods for observing that structural invariance persists despite differences in orientation, projection and appearance.
